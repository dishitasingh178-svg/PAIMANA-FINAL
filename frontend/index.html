01 · The problem

A large portfolio, running late and over budget, and the signals arrive too late.

Central infrastructure projects are reported once a month in long PDF flash reports. By the time a cost revision or a slipped commissioning date is noticed in those tables, the overrun has usually already happened. Reviewers need to know which projects are drifting now.

02 · The PAIMANA solution

From the monthly flash report to a scored, searchable project database.

An administrator uploads each official flash report. PAIMANA extracts every project row, stores it as a dated snapshot, and runs the risk models on it straight away, so the portfolio view is current the moment a report lands.

  1. Input

    Flash report PDF

    The official monthly project flash report, uploaded by an administrator.

  2. Extraction

    Tables to fields

    Project, cost, schedule and milestone columns are parsed and normalised.

  3. Database

    Project snapshots

    Each report month becomes a stored update for every project it covers.

  4. Machine learning

    Three risk models

    Cost and schedule XGBoost models plus a Cox survival model score each project.

  5. Risk

    Composite 0–100

    Scores combine into one composite and a Low, Watch, Elevated or Critical tier.

  6. Alerts

    Early warnings

    The warning engine raises prioritised signals for review.

03 · Predictive intelligence

Three models, one composite risk score.

Each project is scored for the two failures that matter most, cost overrun and delay, plus how its completion risk evolves over its lifecycle. The weighted result is a single 0–100 score that can be compared across the whole portfolio.

Schedule XGBoost

Cox survival model

04 · Risk detection

Four tiers that say how much attention a project needs.

The composite score places every project in one of four bands. Counts below are the current portfolio, taken from each project's latest prediction.

05 · Early warning

Signals, ranked by priority, with the reason attached.

When a new report moves a project's score or crosses a tier, the early-warning engine raises a signal. Each one says what changed, why it was flagged and what to look at first, and it stays open until a reviewer acts on it.
